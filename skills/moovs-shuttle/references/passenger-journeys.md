# Shuttle passenger account and booking journeys

Reviewed 2026-10-16 from complete public Help bodies:
[Passenger account](https://intercom.help/moovs-05c940f1970e/en/articles/9269827-create-and-manage-a-shuttle-passenger-account)
for account access, and
[Passenger booking](https://intercom.help/moovs-05c940f1970e/en/articles/9302607-book-and-manage-a-shuttle-trip-as-a-passenger)
for the three booking modes below. These are prose-backed procedures, not current
UI observation, entitlement or successful checkout verification.

Coach the passenger using their own provider-supplied company link/identity, or
help the operator relay instructions. Operator administration is a
[separate workflow](bookings.md); neither operator nor assistant impersonates a
passenger to test booking. Passenger performs every account edit, verification,
booking, self-check-in, cancellation and purchase. Pause inspection for credentials,
codes and payment entry; never request these, QR passes or secure links in chat.
Use only the requested account/route section and reuse known details. Payment or
account creation is not required merely to explain a journey.

## Create and access a Shuttle passenger account

1. Open the exact company link or QR destination provided by the transportation
   provider. Use the offered experience; do not require an app installation. The
   account Help describes browser access; a different company's link may show
   different routes/sign-in.
2. If a new account is needed and creation is offered, passenger selects that
   option and enters requested details privately: these can include name, phone,
   email and company. Complete the displayed verification, review/complete the
   profile, then continue to the available route or booking experience.
3. For an existing account, use the sign-in method actually shown. In standard
   phone sign-in, passenger enters their mobile number, requests the verification
   code, then enters the texted six-digit code privately. Email or company sign-in can
   differ; follow the offered instructions, not an invented password/SSO flow.
4. Recheck the intended company and available account/route view without making a
   booking. Account/profile menu can show personal details and offered updates.
   Passenger performs any requested update and reopens the profile to compare it.
   Successful sign-in is not proof of eligibility for every route/client program.
5. To end the session, passenger chooses **Log out**, not account deletion.
   Deletion is a different consequential action; the source does not establish
   its confirmation/recovery effects. Do not invent a deletion, reset or merge
   sequence. For access failure use the existing
   [access troubleshooting](bookings.md#troubleshoot-passenger-access) rather than
   another company's login or repeated account creation.

## Book a Stop Time Select ride as a passenger

This is the passenger's Fixed Schedule **Stop Time Select** experience, not the
Operator Stop-to-Stop creation flow or administrative pass configuration.

1. Passenger signs in through the intended provider link and opens an available
   route. If tickets/package purchase is needed, the public guide says choose
   the quantity and purchase; passenger reviews the actual offer, terms and any
   payment privately. Exact package checkout and validity rules are not given;
   ask the provider at an unsupported choice rather than inventing them. An
   existing suitable ticket needs no additional purchase merely for this guide.
2. Select **Book a Ride**, then the intended route, date, stop and available time.
   Confirm whether this ride is for the passenger or another person; passenger
   reviews the details and submits only their intended real booking.
3. Open **Upcoming Trips** or **Past Trips**, as appropriate, and compare the
   intended booking/date/time/stop/person. A submit action alone is not readback.
4. Self check-in is optional only if that booking offers it, when actually ready
   to board. If cancellation is offered and wanted, passenger reviews its actual
   confirmation before proceeding, then checks the booking/ticket result.
   Cancellation can return a ticket, but does not automatically refund money
   paid for a ticket/package. Do not promise a refund or invent cancellation terms.

This ordinary ticket-to-ride path does not establish remaining-use decrement,
pass creation/activation, eligibility, expiry or administrative changes/refunds.
The [pass administration/remaining-use gap](passenger-capabilities.md#ticket-passes-and-remaining-uses)
remains separate; a successful booking does not close it.

## Book Stop-to-Stop tickets as a passenger

Use the provider's Fixed Schedule **Stop-to-Stop Search**, not Stop Time Select
or the Operator booking screen. Guest access exists only when offered.

1. Select **Book Tickets**, then **One-way** or **Round Trip**. Choose **From** and
   **To** stops and the relevant departure/return dates.
2. Enter passenger types/quantities and choose an available trip. Passenger
   supplies required names/contact, notes and any offered additional items.
3. Choose gratuity if offered/required, review the terms and total, then passenger
   accepts terms and selects **Confirm & Pay** only for the intended real purchase.
   Sensitive payment entry stays private; neither assistant nor operator checks
   out in the passenger's identity. A search or price review need not reach payment.
4. Signed-in passengers review current/past trips in **My Bookings**. If available,
   a guest can receive a secure booking link; do not assume it was delivered or
   ask them to paste it in chat. Compare the actual confirmation/booking's trip,
   stops, dates, counts and total; confirmation is not proof of bank settlement.
5. Changes or cancellation of a paid Stop-to-Stop booking go to the transportation
   provider. Do not substitute Stop Time Select's optional cancellation/ticket
   return, change paid service details as a test or promise an automatic refund.

## Book Shared Shuttle as a passenger

1. Through the provider's Shared Shuttle experience, enter eligible pickup and
   drop-off locations and travel date. Add requested passenger types, luggage,
   notes and ADA information; airport service requests flight information.
2. Review the requested service time and fare, then enter the booking contact.
   Entered flight information does not establish current tracking or automatically
   reschedule service. Resolve actual validation/eligibility with the provider,
   not by inventing an address, flight, route or date to force a result.
3. Passenger completes the offered checkout privately for the intended real
   booking. The guide does not establish a universal final button label; stop
   before an unclear consequential step rather than transplant **Confirm & Pay**
   from Stop-to-Stop. Do not make a test payment or assume booking means assignment.
4. Keep the booking's actual confirmation or tracking link privately. Compare
   intended date, locations, passenger count, requested time/fare and any actual
   booking/payment state available. When offered, the link can show trip/driver
   information and provider contact details; its existence does not prove live
   location freshness, driver assignment or message delivery.
5. Contact the transportation provider for changes/cancellation. Do not create a
   duplicate booking after an uncertain checkout; inspect the existing result
   with the provider first. Payment and refund outcomes need their own evidence.

For any booked ride, passenger checks travel details/provider instructions and
keeps any supplied ticket/confirmation/QR pass available for boarding. Continuous
Loop instead has [map/readiness guidance](passenger-capabilities.md#review-continuous-loop-live-tracking-readiness)
and requires no seat booking. If the actual page differs materially, obtain
current supported guidance; do not reconstruct a plausible checkout screen.
